# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

C++ Core is an open-source project, and everyone interacting with it is expected to treat others with respect, regardless of their experience, background, or circumstances.

**Being more experienced does not make you more important. Being less experienced does not make you less deserving of respect.**

This applies to everyone involved in the project, including contributors, maintainers, people asking questions, people reporting bugs, and people who are still learning how to contribute to open-source software.

## Expected behaviour

Everyone participating in this project is expected to:

- **Treat people with equal respect, regardless of experience.** New developers, students, first-time contributors, and people unfamiliar with particular tools or conventions deserve the same basic courtesy as experienced professionals. Do not use someone's inexperience as a reason to dismiss, ridicule, patronise, or exclude them.
- **Explain rather than belittle.** If someone makes a mistake, misunderstands something, or submits an imperfect contribution, address the actual problem. Explain what needs to change when appropriate. Do not turn an opportunity to teach into an opportunity to demonstrate superiority.
- **Criticise work, not personal worth.** Technical disagreements, direct criticism, and rejection of contributions are all acceptable. Insults, humiliation, condescension, and attacks on someone's intelligence or competence are not.
- **Treat people fairly regardless of personal characteristics or background.** Prejudice, discrimination, harassment, and exclusion based on identity, origin, nationality, ethnicity, race, religion, age, sex, disability, socioeconomic circumstances, or any other personal characteristic are not acceptable.
- **Respect different levels of access and opportunity.** Not everyone has the same equipment, education, internet access, professional experience, or exposure to programming. Do not assume that someone's circumstances reflect their ability, effort, or potential.
- **Engage in good faith.** Disagreement is welcome. Deliberately misrepresenting someone's position, provoking them, or using discussions to assert personal dominance is not.
- **Respect people's time and boundaries.** Do not pressure contributors into unpaid work, demand immediate responses, or treat participation as an obligation.
- **Accept accountability.** If someone explains that your conduct has been harmful or inappropriate, consider the criticism seriously and make a reasonable effort to address it.

## Unacceptable behaviour

The following behaviours are not acceptable in project spaces:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or prejudice against any person or group.
- Gatekeeping based on experience, credentials, qualifications, employment, education, or familiarity with particular technologies.
- Treating beginner questions as inherently stupid, wasting people's time, or evidence that they do not belong in programming.
- Mocking people for mistakes, basic misunderstandings, imperfect English, unfamiliarity with open-source conventions, or the quality of an early contribution.
- Using seniority, technical knowledge, professional status, or contribution history to claim that another person deserves less respect or has less right to participate.
- Deliberately discouraging newcomers, making them feel unwelcome, or setting unreasonable expectations to prevent them from contributing.
- Dismissing someone's contribution without considering its substance because of who they are, where they come from, or how much experience they have.
- Unwanted sexual attention, demeaning remarks, persistent personal contact after a request to stop, or other forms of harassment.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Retaliating against anyone who raises a concern or participates in an investigation.

This list is not exhaustive. Conduct that violates the spirit of these standards may also be addressed.

## Technical standards are not personal hierarchies

C++ Core values technical accuracy, maintainability, security, and thoughtful design. Contributions may be rejected, code may require substantial revision, and incorrect claims may be challenged directly.

None of this requires disrespect.

A contributor is not entitled to have their work accepted simply because they submitted it. Equally, a maintainer is not entitled to demean someone simply because they have the authority to reject it. Standards apply to the work; basic respect applies to the person.

Experience should help people contribute more effectively and help others learn. It should never become an excuse to establish a hierarchy of human worth.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. They may remove inappropriate content, close discussions, reject contributions, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently exclude someone from project spaces when warranted by the severity or persistence of their behaviour.

Responses will take the circumstances into account, including the nature of the conduct, its impact, whether it was repeated, and whether the person involved makes a genuine effort to address it. Serious misconduct may warrant immediate action.

Maintainers are expected to follow this Code of Conduct themselves. Their position does not exempt them from its requirements.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project spaces, including the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and other channels used for C++ Core activities. It also applies in other settings when someone's conduct is directly connected to their participation in the project.

## Final note

C++ Core began as a personal project to learn C++. It is open-source because someone else might find it useful, learn from it, or help improve it.

People should be able to participate in that process without being made to feel inferior for being new, different, or still learning. Nobody arrives knowing everything, and nobody's experience gives them the right to make someone else feel small.

**Contribute in good faith. Disagree respectfully. Help people learn. Never confuse knowing more about programming with being worth more as a person.**
